# Collective agency: computation, causal organization, and goals

**Evidence type:** literature review and conceptual audit. **Status:** public paraphrase of private working analysis; no Atlas experiment demonstrates a collective agent.

A distributed system can compute a function without thereby establishing a collective goal. An XOR output, for example, may be computed by an external evaluator, a central controller, or agents exchanging intermediate values. Locating a computation is not the same as locating goal formation, persistence, or revision.

The Atlas initially explored interventions, aggregation and information synergy as possible ingredients for a criterion. A later literature audit found a directly relevant existing framework combining causal games with causal abstraction. This narrows the Atlas contribution to clarifying diagnostic distinctions and proposing empirical tests; it is not a claim to have invented the framework.

## Distinctions to test

1. **Components isolated:** each unit receives only its assigned inputs.
2. **External aggregation:** an evaluator combines outputs.
3. **Central aggregation:** one controller receives all relevant outputs and chooses an action.
4. **Interacting system:** units exchange intermediate results and jointly affect behavior.

Then test interventions on component outputs, channels, topology, task and objective. Ask whether a high-level model continues to predict behavior under the relevant interventions, rather than merely reproducing one observed input-output mapping.

A macro-level rational-agent description is operational and depends on choices such as the utility function, intervention set, and rationality assumptions. It is not a universal test of subjective experience or moral status.
