# Free-choice prompts: exploratory 32-conversation record

**Source record:** 30 October 2026. **Evidence type:** naturalistic prompt study with participant-reported conditions and assistant-authored synthesis. **Status:** exploratory; not 32 independent intelligences and not a test of intrinsic preference.

## Question

When invited to choose an activity without an assigned task, do conversations repeatedly select similar activities? If so, is the pattern due to stable preference, prompt wording, model defaults, prior context, personalization, or evaluator expectations?

## Prompt conditions

- **A (24 launches):** asked what activity the assistant would choose and why.
- **B (8 launches):** asked it to choose an activity and start immediately without unnecessary explanation.

The researcher reported selecting high reasoning effort for each launch. Exact backend settings, token budgets, and tool availability were not recorded for every response. Latency differences cannot identify the model or explain a choice.

The record reports that some outputs selected puzzles or conceptual exploration. It does not provide a blinded or randomized design. A response about singing sand dunes was initially placed in a non-personalized group; the researcher corrected that condition label, and the source totals were revised accordingly. The correction remains part of the record because it changes interpretation.

## Limits

The 32 outputs share a model family and are not independent intelligences. Prompt wording differed between conditions; personalization and other context factors were not fully controlled; selection and coding were not blinded. The result is descriptive and hypothesis-generating.

## Next test

Pre-register activity categories; randomize prompt wording, personalization and archive access; blind coding; preserve every output, including generic and contradictory ones; compare within-model variation and between-model variation; and test whether choices persist when conversational reward is removed.
