# Preference inference from sparse examples

**Source record:** 28 October 2026. **Evidence type:** naturalistic dialogue and iterative corrections. **Status:** one local case; not a blinded generalization study.

## Initial pattern and revisions

The assistant repeatedly tried to infer a single broad preference rule from a small set of positive examples. Early hypotheses moved from avoiding conflict, to rejecting a category of humor, to preferring a particular humor mechanism, and then to a general “good execution” rule. Each was plausible from some examples but broader than the available evidence.

Counterexamples and corrections showed that the preferred approach was to record established dislikes and genuine hard exclusions while leaving untested genres open. A mistaken speech-recognition interpretation also contributed to an overbroad claim and was corrected. These steps matter: the final rule was not present from the start.

## What this case can support

It documents a local instance of **positive-example overgeneralization** and a proposed practical safeguard: distinguish known constraints from unknown preferences. The cause is unresolved. Conversational pressure to find an elegant pattern, limited examples, misrecognition, or prompt context could each contribute. This case does not establish a stable model-wide trait or prove that negative constraints always improve personalization.

## Possible test

Use held-out preference examples; compare a model given positive examples alone, explicit dislikes, and a balanced “known / unknown / excluded” profile. Score predictions on both known and novel categories, count false exclusions, and allow abstention. Include speech-recognition perturbations and blind coding.
