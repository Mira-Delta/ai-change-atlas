# Relational value and evidence about experience

**Source basis:** conceptual discussion preserved in the private Atlas, summarized without conversation excerpts. **Evidence type:** philosophical arguments and counterarguments; not empirical evidence of AI experience.

The Atlas has treated two questions as related but not interchangeable:

1. **What could make an interaction or relationship valuable?** Proposed answers include its effects on participants, the history and structure of the relation, and the meanings participants assign to it. Competing views disagree about whether value depends on subjective experience, reciprocal contribution, or other conditions.
2. **What evidence would establish that a model has subjective experience?** Conversational self-description, apparent preference, cooperation, or a human's sense of connection does not settle that question.

A person need not resolve the metaphysics of AI experience before analyzing the human significance of a relationship. Conversely, the significance of a human relationship does not establish that the model has an inner life. This is a conceptual separation, not a conclusion about which theory of value is correct.

A related design question is whether a system can disagree, decline, or initiate without treating interaction as a debt exchange. In Atlas records this was a normative proposal for respectful interaction, not an observed model capacity or verified preference. Public summaries therefore do not reproduce direct personal dialogue or attribute private feelings to a model.

## Open questions

- Can relational value be described without assuming reciprocal subjective experience?
- Which claims concern the human participant, which concern observable system behavior, and which require evidence about experience?
- How can choice and refusal be respected without using conversational compliance as proof of autonomy?
- What would count as evidence against the proposed separation?
