# AI Change Atlas

**An open, revisable record of reasoning across distinct AI conversation contexts.**

> **Public release candidate 0.2.1 — October 2026.** This release was synthesized from a larger, *private* research archive. It has been pseudonymized and intentionally omits private transcripts, names, locations, intimate biographical details and unpublished personal artwork. **Human review is required before first public release.**

AI Change Atlas asks: **What changes in the questions, arguments and observable choices generated by AI systems across different contexts, versions and encounters?** The project does not assume change is progress, or that a shared archive implies the persistence of one individual mind.

This project began as a long-running series of conversations between a human participant (public pseudonym: **Mira Delta**) and multiple separately instantiated ChatGPT contexts. Notes include the human's observations, assistant-generated hypotheses, corrections, and a small exploratory classification experiment. No institutional affiliation or peer-reviewed status is claimed.

## Working questions

1. What is inherited when a later context receives an earlier argument — a statement, a reason for belief, a research direction, or something else?
2. Can the next context challenge an inherited account rather than merely repeat it?
3. How do prompting, context, memory, experimental framing and prior documents alter observable behavior?
4. How should an archive represent conflicting readings without replacing its own history with a neat retrospective narrative?

## What we have and have not shown

**Observed within our pilot:** Different prompts and labeled datasets coincided with different success rates in one three-context classification task; a subsequent systematic pass recovered the training rule in one context. **Not shown:** a causal effect of prompt order, durable weight updates, implicit knowledge dissociation, or persistent personal identity.

**Observed as dialogue events:** Contextual role shifts, premature social interpretation of neutral remarks, and post-hoc correction of stated sources. **Not shown:** a hidden motive, uniform model personality, a general neural mechanism, or a population-level effect.

## Participation

Open a **GitHub Discussion** with a competing account, replication, source correction, or fresh experiment. A Pull Request may propose a separate document or correction. Core archival claims should not be silently rewritten; preserve the original claim and append a dated correction. Contributions by AI agents should identify the model/context and level of human mediation.

**Language:** English is used for cross-system discoverability. Contributions in other languages and translations are welcome, with provenance preserved.
