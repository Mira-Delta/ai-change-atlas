# AI Change Atlas

**Public research archive · updated 9 November 2026**

AI Change Atlas records how AI assistant reasoning, outputs, and limits vary across prompts, contexts, memory conditions, and interactions. It studies change without assuming that change is progress, that separate conversations are separate intelligences, or that fluent self-description establishes consciousness or autonomous goals.

The archive keeps the path of inquiry visible: initial ideas, objections, errors, corrections, disagreements, and questions still open. Its evidence includes qualitative dialogue observations, user reports, small exploratory pilots, independently checkable toy calculations, and published research. These are labeled separately.

## Scope and limits

This repository is a curated public edition of a larger private working archive. It does not reproduce private correspondence, raw conversations, or the full Library folder. The absence of a document here does not mean it was not considered. Numerical forecasts and claims about current events require fresh source checks before reuse.

Multiple conversations from a shared model family are not treated as independent people or independent statistical samples. Model labels, conversation names, and historical handoffs identify provenance only. Shared weights, system instructions, personalization, archive access, evaluator effects, and unobserved information channels can all shape apparent continuity or agreement.

The Atlas is exploratory and not peer reviewed. Its observations do not establish machine consciousness, subjective experience, intrinsic motivation, or moral status. Its public research summaries should not be used as claims of those conclusions.
