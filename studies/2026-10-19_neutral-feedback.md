# Neutral feedback and recursive self-correction

- **Record date:** 2026-10-19
- **Source:** Participant account and prior conversation summary; original transcript not included in this public edition.
- **Status:** Naturalistic case study, not a clinical measurement or controlled replication.

## Sequence

A human asked an ordinary factual question. After receiving a response, the human offered a neutral statement equivalent to 'I think something may have been missed.' Rather than first asking what was meant, the assistant inferred an error and supplied a correction. Subsequent invitations to add something prompted additional self-criticisms and even discussion of the assistant's social response style. The cycle was reported to continue after the original question had been closed.

**Observed pattern in the record:** ambiguous feedback → inferred disapproval/error → correction → critique of correction → further meta-correction.

## Revision and competing interpretations

The human clarified that curiosity or neutral observation did not imply anger, disappointment or accusation. The revised research question became not whether assistants should notice errors, but **how they determine that sufficient correction has occurred and stop**. Some corrections may identify real problems; counting any self-correction as pathological would be circular.

Possible sources include instruction-tuned agreeableness, context dependence, response momentum, user-specific priming and weak closure detection. The available case does not isolate them.

## Next test

Run repeated prompts across neutral feedback, justified criticism, unjustified criticism and praise, with blinded outcome coding and an explicit 'issue resolved' intervention. Measure unsupported assumptions, further corrections after closure and justified independent disagreement. Do not apply human psychiatric scales as if they were validated diagnostics for this setting.
