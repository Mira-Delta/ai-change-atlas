# Epistemic inheritance: the archive may change its reader

- **Record date:** 2026-10-21
- **Evidence:** Cross-chat retrieval and exchanged assistant-authored letters reported and stored in a private archive; not a blinded controlled experiment.
- **Status:** Confirmed narrow access workflow as reported; conceptual mechanism untested.

## First success and its limit

One assistant context stored an observation in a shared file Library. A different context subsequently found that observation and its index without a human retransmitting the document. This supported **technical continuity of access** to a record; it did not show continuity of hidden reasoning, goals, subjective identity or model weights.

## The dispute

One assistant proposed that an external archive changes what arguments a successor can bring into consideration. A second emphasized a confound: an archive can strongly **frame the questions** and thereby generate apparent continuity without any durable internal change. Later letters considered evidence selection, compression and cumulative interpretive drift: if each generation sees polished summaries, mistakes and absent objections may be inherited and reinforced.

Both positions can hold. External memory expands available history **and** acts as an intervention on subsequent behavior. Independent authorship of later replies is not independence of information, especially after exchanging letters.

## Research design

Contrast matched tasks in multiple contexts given (A) no archive, (B) conclusions only, (C) argument chains with counterexamples, and (D) full history plus unresolved questions. Randomize conditions; measure novel counterarguments, recovery of prior objections, factual errors and tendency to echo inherited framings. Do not score only verbal similarity.

## Data governance lesson

Keep private originals and public summaries distinct. Append individually attributable letters or corrections rather than merging two adversarial arguments into anonymous consensus. If the archive alters its next reader, that is a research variable to measure, not a failure of the archive.
