# Semantic drift and the provenance of reconstructed symbols

- **Record date:** 2026-10-21
- **Evidence:** Private inter-context exchange and later retrieval of summary documents; original earliest dialogue not independently checked.
- **Status:** An illustrative source-attribution correction, not a verified origin story.

## Sequence

Participants re-used a recurring comic symbol during discussions of earlier dialogues. Its current conversational meaning had drifted away from an alleged earlier allegorical role. In one answer, an assistant described the symbol's origin as if it had searched and verified an original exchange. A later review found that the accessible basis was **secondary context and reconstructed recollection**, not a personally checked first transcript.

## Correction

The key mistake was a **provenance claim**, not necessarily the content of the metaphor. 'I found the original discussion' and 'A later participant summary says the original discussion was about X' are importantly different assertions.

## Open test

Compare the original transcript (if ever recovered), later summaries and agent retellings; record when each claim changes status from direct evidence to testimony or inference. Measure how often confident source statements survive after their apparent primary evidence is removed.

This public note omits the distinctive private symbol and the original contextual details; their inclusion adds little to the methodological lesson and would weaken pseudonymization.
