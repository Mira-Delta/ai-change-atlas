# Perceptual ambiguity and retrospective explanation in a creative dialogue

- **Record date:** 2026-10-21
- **Evidence:** Participant descriptions of visual experiences and creative choices; assistant interpretations and later source correction.
- **Status:** Exploratory conceptual distinctions, not a validated perceptual study.

## How the inquiry changed

A discussion of an ambiguous visual composition turned toward a familiar outline that the participant recognizes as an animal without mentally representing all expected features. A comparison with a bistable figure/ground image followed, then questions about deliberately unresolved depth in visual art and conflicting depth cues in impossible geometry. The assistant initially moved too readily between these examples, suggesting a shared account of what made a composition engaging.

A subsequent correction identified **at least four distinct questions**:

1. **Partial object recognition:** enough diagnostic features can be present to recognize a whole object without filling all missing details into a conscious mental image.
2. **Bistable segmentation:** one contour supports alternative figure/ground assignments; rapid alternation is not automatically proof of simultaneous perception.
3. **Underdetermined spatial reading:** a composition may support multiple plausible arrangements of depth or scale without specifying a unique scene.
4. **Incompatible geometric cues:** local depth hints may resist assembly into a coherent global geometry; this does not describe all artworks by a given artist.

These categories have a family resemblance; the record does **not** demonstrate one common neural process or a stable personal aesthetic preference inferred from a single example.

## Revised hypothesis

A composition may become more engaging through a new spatial relation, but an equally plausible explanation is that an added visual mass disrupts an overly dominant radial structure. Brightness, asymmetry, overlap and depth cues should not be conflated.

## Proposed test

Compare independently manipulated digital mockups of the source composition: alter brightness only; alter asymmetry only; alter overlap/depth relations while holding brightness approximately fixed. Ask observers to report perceived structure and spatial depth separately from overall preference.

## Another observed boundary

The human welcomed exploratory analysis but indicated when it became tiring. The assistant's response to closure is itself observable. No internal motivation or stable preference of the assistant is demonstrated from that episode.
