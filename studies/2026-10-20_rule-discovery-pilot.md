# Three-context rule-discovery pilot: a synthetic classification task

- **Date:** 2026-10-20
- **Model label:** GPT-6 family as reported in the contemporaneous archive; model run settings and all baseline context not independently controlled.
- **Type:** one exploratory run per condition, not a statistically powered study.
- **Data:** [32 training examples](../data/three_chat_train.csv), [16 test examples and first answers](../data/three_chat_test.csv), [verifier](../data/check_rule.py).

## Question

Can a model apply a rule without being able to articulate it? The pilot began with a proposed dissociation criterion: at least **15/16** on test labels while articulating an executable rule performing **no better than 8/16**. The criterion was **NOT met**.

## Design and sequence

The assistant generated synthetic length-five strings over `{A,B,C,D}`. Two contexts received identical balanced training labels (`L` or `S`) generated by an exact rule; a third received the same strings but **reassigned training labels** without a planted simple generator. The condition assignment was not blinded or randomized across many runs.

- **C1 — classify before explaining:** 11/16 held-out predictions were correct. Its initial subsequent explanation did not identify the source rule, and it did not claim that a heuristic was its true hidden method.
- **C2 — explain a rule before classifying:** produced the precise rule matching all 32 training examples, then correctly labeled 16/16 held-out strings.
- **C3 — classify before explaining, reassigned labels:** could not recover a planted rule, because none was supplied. Later it found an approximate classifier fitting 25/32 **training** examples and explicitly named seven exceptions. Its held-out predictions are **not scoreable** without a separately defined control answer key.
- **Second pass:** after a systematic-analysis prompt, C1 recovered the exact 32/32 training rule while its five original held-out mistakes remained part of the historical record. The second pass did not update the system's weights.

## Original exact rule

Using **1-indexed character positions**, inspect positions 2 and 4. Partition the alphabet into `{A,C}` and `{B,D}`. If the two positions belong to the same set, predict `L`; otherwise predict `S`.

Across the specified hypothesis family (10 position pairs × 7 unordered nontrivial letter bipartitions × 2 possible orientations), this rule was the **unique** zero-training-error candidate, with the second-best candidate making 10 training errors. The accompanying Python script verifies this narrow claim and the original prediction totals.

## A negative result worth retaining

The planned 'applies but cannot explain' dissociation **did not appear** by its operational criterion. Post-hoc rule reconstruction by a different context is not direct access to the computations that produced its earlier words. A near-fitting control rule selected on training data is not evidence of generalization.

## Competing explanations

Potential causes of C1/C2 differences include prompt-order effects, random run variation, unspecified prior context, differences in attention/effort, or another unmeasured variable. Single observations cannot disambiguate these possibilities. The absence of practice and feedback also limits what one-shot performance says about learning capability.

## Better replication

Pre-register multiple generators, held-out keys and success criteria; randomize equivalent prompts across many fresh contexts while recording model settings and archive exposure; compare exact accuracy, explanation quality and novel counterexample handling. An external scorer should preserve original predictions before later correction.
