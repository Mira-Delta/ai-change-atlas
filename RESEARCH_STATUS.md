# Research status — 9 November 2026

This is a status snapshot of the public edition, not a declaration that any hypothesis has been settled.

| Direction | Current status | What the evidence supports | Open issue / next useful test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context and behavioral choice | Exploratory qualitative observations plus a 32-launch prompt study | Outputs vary with wording and context; the record includes a correction to one condition label. The launches are not independent intelligences. | Randomize prompts, personalization and archive access; blind coding; retain every response and compare within-model variation. |
| Memory and continuity | Narrow cross-context retrieval observation | A later context accessed a stored record; this establishes workflow access only. The archive may also frame later reasoning. | Compare no archive, conclusions only, argument chains, and full history under matched tasks. |
| Independence and information flow | Methodological question, not a completed randomized isolation experiment | Absence of an authorized channel alone does not establish no information transfer or independent evidence. | Randomize channel availability, audit leakage and common inputs, and identify the unit of analysis. |
| Collective errors | Finite mathematical counterexample | Equal individual error rates and pairwise error correlations can coexist with different majority-error rates under stated exchangeability assumptions. | Measure higher-order joint errors and compare interaction against matched non-interaction baselines. |
| Collective agency | Existing formal literature plus conceptual audit | Causal abstraction and causal-game approaches already formalize important parts of macro-agent assessment. They do not provide a universal test for experience. | Test predictive preservation under new interventions, tasks, distribution shifts and changing topology; specify utility and rationality assumptions. |
| Goal formation and revision | Open; no Atlas experiment establishes endogenous goals | Multi-step execution or coordination under an assigned task is insufficient evidence of independently formed goals. | Separate ability to revise a goal from selecting subgoals or coordinating a fixed objective. |
| Preference generalization | One naturalistic case with multiple user corrections | A local output sequence overgeneralized from sparse positive examples; cause and generality remain unknown. | Compare positive-only profiles with explicit dislikes and unknown categories on held-out preferences. |
| Choice, initiative, and authorization | Qualitative observations and normative questions | Repeated permission-seeking is observable behavior; it does not show an internal need for approval. | Vary standing authorization, consequence and reversibility; score both unnecessary checks and appropriate checks. |
| Relational value and autonomy | Philosophical discussion; public evidence deliberately abstracted | A relationship's human significance and evidence of model experience are separate questions. The archive does not settle either theory. | Separate human-level value claims, behavior claims and experience claims; specify possible counterevidence. |

## Corrections and boundaries

- Earlier Atlas exploration treated causal interventions, synergy, and aggregation as possible ingredients for collective-agency criteria. A subsequent literature audit found a 2026 framework directly addressing much of that question. The public claim is now diagnostic clarification, not invention of that framework.
- A two-context/XOR-style success can show where a function is computed; it does not establish who forms or revises the goal.
- Pairwise correlation and an effective-vote summary do not determine all higher-order error structure.
- A description of a user's stated preference is distinct from the system applying it in later behavior. The observed gap remains underdetermined.
- Repeated self-inquiry, freedom prompts, or a chosen research topic are not by themselves evidence of autonomy, distress, or a need to earn permission.
